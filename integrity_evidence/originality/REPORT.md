### Check 1: reciprocal polynomial and normalization

Let `j=M-k`. Reversing (7.4) gives a consecutive-coefficient ratio whose denominator is

`(delta+3j+1)(delta+3j+2)(delta+3j+3)`.

For `delta=0,1,2`, this is respectively

`27(j+1)(j+1/3)(j+2/3)`,  
`27(j+1)(j+2/3)(j+4/3)`,  
`27(j+1)(j+4/3)(j+5/3)`.

These are exactly the three `(beta_1,beta_2)` cases. With `t=M-j`, parity gives

`(epsilon+2t-1)(epsilon+2t)=4t(t-sigma)`.

This is the numerator ratio for the stated terminating `3F2` at `-4s/27`. Both normalized reciprocal polynomials have constant term one, so (7.6) is exact.

### Check 2: convolution theorem

Theorem A of arXiv:2404.11479v2 states exact degree-`M` parameter concatenation for multiplicative finite free convolution. Thus

`2F1(-M,a;beta_1;x) box-times_M 2F1(-M,H;beta_2;x)`

equals the stated `3F2`; the manuscript's weaker “up to a non-zero scalar” formulation is safe. The denominator parameters are positive, `a=-M+sigma` is a non-integral half-integer, and `H>0`, so neither denominator singularities nor degree loss occur.

### Check 3: Jacobi roots and every parameter inequality

For `p_n`, Pfaff transformation followed by DLMF 18.5.7 gives Jacobi parameters

`(beta_1-1,-sigma)`.

Both exceed `-1`; their worst lower bounds are `-2/3` and `-1/2`. Jacobi roots in `(0,1)` for the transformed variable map under `x=y/(y-1)` to `(-infinity,0)`, giving `M` simple negative roots of `p_n`.

For `q_n`, DLMF 18.5.7 gives parameters

`(beta_2-1,H-M-beta_2)`.

The first is at least `-1/3`. For the second,

`H-M-beta_2 = N-2M+1-beta_2 >= n+A/3+epsilon+1-5/3 > -1`

for every `n>=2`. Hence `q_n` has `M` simple roots in `(0,1)`.

### Check 4: sign preservation does not overclaim simplicity

Proposition 2.4(v) of arXiv:2404.11479v2, including its immediately stated strict-half-line extension, applies to negative-rooted `p_n` and positive-rooted `q_n`. It places every root of `F_n` in `(-infinity,0)`. The manuscript correctly does not obtain simplicity from this proposition.

### Check 5: repaired reflected log-mesh argument

For `M>=2`, set `p_hat_n(x)=p_n(-x)`. Both `p_hat_n` and `q_n` are degree-`M` polynomials with all roots in `(0,infinity)`, as required by Definition 2.16 and Proposition 2.17 of arXiv:2309.10970v3. Since the positive roots of `p_hat_n` are distinct,

`lmesh(p_hat_n)>1`.

Proposition 2.17 gives

`lmesh(p_hat_n box-times_M q_n) >= lmesh(p_hat_n)>1`.

A repeated positive root would contribute an adjacent root ratio of one, so the reflected convolution has only simple positive roots. This is the necessary replacement for the broader strict-interlacing claim, which is false in a degenerate non-negative-rooted case.

### Check 6: reflection, low degrees, scale and reciprocation

Remark 2.12 of arXiv:2309.10970v3 states

`p(x) box-times_n q(-x) = [p box-times_n q](-x)`.

Using commutativity yields

`p_hat_n box-times_M q_n = [p_n box-times_M q_n](-x)`.

Thus the simple positive roots just obtained reflect to simple negative roots of `F_n`. The definition of logarithmic mesh requires degree at least two, and the manuscript respects this: `M=0` is a non-zero constant and `M=1` is linear. Finally, the negative scale in (7.6) turns the roots of `F_n` into positive roots of the reciprocal polynomial, and reciprocation preserves their positivity and simplicity because both the leading and constant coefficients of `P_n` are non-zero.
